The obstacle channel at the top row provides a constant bias because the outer wall is always marked as an obstacle. This pattern is characteristic of a game where objects (both rewards and hazards) are spawned at the top boundary and then move downward into the playable area, while the agent operates mainly in the lower part of the grid. The agent’s own location is not needed for decision‑making (its weights at non‑wall cells are small), suggesting the agent’s vertical position is fixed or irrelevant to the policy. The output layer shows modest biases for all actions, with a slight preference for the no‑op and right actions, and the hidden‑to‑output weights allow the network to strongly drive left, right, up, or down actions depending on the hidden‑unit activations triggered by top‑row observations.  

These observations fit the classic arcade game Breakout: a paddle (the agent) moves horizontally at the bottom to hit a ball (the moving object) that bounces off the top wall and bricks; breaking a brick (reward target) yields reward; the walls (including the top row) are obstacles; and the previous frame helps the network infer the ball’s direction.
